# Spanish notes (ISO code: es)

## Gender & articles
- Two genders; teach nouns with el/la/los/las, un/una. -o usually m, -a usually f; traps: *el día, el mapa, el problema, el idioma (m); la mano, la foto, la moto (f)*; -ción/-dad/-tad → f.
- Feminine nouns starting with stressed a- take *el* in singular: *el agua fría, el aula* (still feminine).
- Adjectives agree in gender/number and usually follow the noun.

## Contractions
- a + el → **al**, de + el → **del** (never with *él* pronoun or proper names like *El Salvador* in writing).

## Idiom traps
- *tener* for age/hunger/cold/fear: *tengo 30 años, tengo hambre*.
- *estar* for location & temporary states (*la panadería está al lado de la estación*); *ser* for events' location (*la fiesta es en mi casa*).
- *perder el tren* (miss the train), *coger/tomar un taxi* (coger is vulgar in much of Latin America - teach *tomar* if the learner targets LatAm).
- Register: tú vs usted; ask whether the learner targets Spain or Latin America (vosotros vs ustedes) and store it in progress.md Preferences.

## Pronunciation trouble spots
- Single tap **r** vs trilled **rr** (*pero / perro*) - Russian speakers usually do rr well, but over-trill single r.
- Pure 5 vowels, no reduction: unstressed *o* stays [o] (Russian speakers tend to reduce to [a]: *coche* not *kache*).
- **b/v** same sound; soft [β] between vowels. **d** between vowels soft [ð] (*cansado*).
- **j/g(e,i)** [x] - close to Russian х ✓. **ll/y** [ʝ]. **h** silent.
- Written accent marks the stressed syllable - stress placement changes meaning (*papa/papá, esta/está*).

## Common errors of Russian/English speakers
- Dropping articles; overusing subject pronouns (*yo*); ser/estar confusion; *gustar* agreement (*me gustan los coches*); wrong past tense choice; *a* + person missing (*veo a María*).
